About Free UK Museums

The UK is one of the few countries in the world where the major national museums are completely free to enter. The British Museum, National Gallery, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Tate Modern and dozens of others charge nothing for their permanent collections.

Why are UK museums free?

Free entry to national museums was introduced in 2001 by the UK government, replacing the previous system of charging for admission. The policy was designed to widen access and increase visitor numbers. It worked — visits to national museums have more than doubled since then.

Best cities for free museums

London has by far the largest concentration — over 20 free museums and galleries, including the British Museum, all three Tate galleries, and the V&A. Edinburgh has four national museums and galleries. Manchester, Liverpool, Cardiff, Oxford, York and Belfast all have excellent free options too.

Free vs paid exhibitions

While permanent collections are free, temporary and special exhibitions usually charge — typically £10-£25 for adults. These often sell out, so book ahead if a specific exhibition is the reason for your visit. Many museums also accept optional donations of £3-£5 at the entrance.

Plan your visit

Most museums are open daily from 10am to 5pm, though some close one day a week (often Monday) and hours vary on public holidays. For booking a trip around a UK bank holiday, see our Bank Holidays Calendar. To plan routes between cities, use our Route Planner.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are UK museums really free?

Yes. Most major UK national museums and galleries offer free entry to their permanent collections every day. Special or temporary exhibitions usually carry a separate charge, typically £10-25.

Which is the most popular free museum in the UK?

The British Museum in London is the most visited free museum in the UK, attracting over 6 million visitors a year. The Natural History Museum, Tate Modern and National Gallery are also among the most popular.

Do I need to book free museums in advance?

Most free museums accept walk-ins. However, in peak season, the British Museum, Natural History Museum and Science Museum recommend booking a free timed-entry ticket online to guarantee entry.

Are free museums open every day?

Most are open daily, typically 10am to 5pm, though some close one day a week (often Monday) and hours can vary on public holidays. Check the museum website before visiting.

What are the best free museums in London?

The best free museums in London include the British Museum, National Gallery, Natural History Museum, Science Museum, Victoria and Albert Museum, Tate Modern, Tate Britain, National Portrait Gallery and Imperial War Museum.

Are there free museums outside London?

Yes — many. Edinburgh has the National Museum of Scotland and three National Galleries. Manchester has Manchester Museum, the Science and Industry Museum and Manchester Art Gallery. Liverpool has the Walker Art Gallery, World Museum and Museum of Liverpool. Cardiff has National Museum Cardiff and St Fagans. Belfast has the Ulster Museum.

Are UK museums free for children?

Yes. Free national museums are free for everyone, including children. Many also run free family activities on weekends and school holidays. Temporary exhibitions may charge for adults but often let children in free.

Do free museums accept donations?

Yes. Most free museums have donation boxes at the entrance and welcome voluntary contributions of £3-£5 per person. Donations are optional but help keep entry free.

Can I take photos in UK museums?

Most UK museums allow photography for personal use without flash. Some temporary exhibitions or loaned objects may be marked 'no photography'. Always check signs or ask staff.

Which free UK museums are best for families?

The Natural History Museum and Science Museum in London, the National Railway Museum in York, the Science and Industry Museum in Manchester, and the Museum of Liverpool are all excellent for families with children.
