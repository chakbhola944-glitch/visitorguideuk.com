Overview

Everyday Practicalities for UK Visitors

Beyond the bigger questions of transport and timing, a handful of everyday practicalities can make a UK trip run more smoothly. The UK uses a distinct three-pin plug type, so visitors from most other countries will need an adapter, and while English is spoken throughout the country, Welsh and Scottish Gaelic remain in use in parts of Wales and Scotland respectively.

Mobile connectivity is generally reliable in cities, with local SIM cards or eSIMs widely available for visitors wanting to avoid roaming charges, and most everyday transactions can be handled with a contactless card rather than cash.

Queuing Culture

Queuing in order is a strong social norm in the UK; jumping a queue is generally frowned upon.

Driving on the Left

The UK drives on the left, which visitors renting a car should be prepared for.

Planning

Time Zone

The UK uses GMT in winter and British Summer Time (BST) in summer, one hour ahead.

More to Consider

Quick Practical Facts

Electrical Plugs

The UK uses a three-pin plug (Type G) at 230V; an adapter is needed for most non-UK devices.

Language

English is spoken throughout the UK, alongside Welsh in parts of Wales and Scottish Gaelic in parts of Scotland.

Emergency Number

999 (or the EU-wide 112) reaches police, fire and ambulance services anywhere in the UK.

Driving Side

Traffic drives on the left, relevant for any visitor planning to hire a car.

Practical

Tips for Travel Tips

  • Pack or buy a UK plug adapter before you rely on charging devices.
  • Consider a local eSIM or SIM card on arrival if you'll need reliable data throughout your trip.
  • Queue in order at bus stops, ticket counters and attractions; it's a strongly observed social norm.
  • Remember the emergency number is 999 (or 112) anywhere in the UK.
