Which Month Should You Visit?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the warmest month in the UK?

July is typically the warmest month in the UK, with average highs of 22-24°C (72-75°F) in London and 19-20°C (66-68°F) in Edinburgh. August is a close second.

What is the coldest month in the UK?

January and February are typically the coldest months, with average lows of 2-3°C (36-37°F) in London and around 0-1°C (32-34°F) in Edinburgh.

What is the wettest month in the UK?

October through January are typically the wettest months across most of the UK. November often has the highest rainfall totals, though it varies significantly by region.

What is the driest month in the UK?

April and May are often the driest months across most of the UK, with lower rainfall and improving temperatures — which is why late spring is a popular time to visit.

What is the best month to visit the UK?

June and September are often considered the best months — long daylight hours, mild-to-warm temperatures, and fewer crowds than peak July-August. May and early October are also good choices.

Does it rain more in Scotland or England?

Scotland is generally wetter than England, particularly the west coast and Highlands. Glasgow receives around 1,100-1,200mm of rain per year, compared with around 600-700mm in London.

How cold does it get in the UK in winter?

UK winter temperatures typically range from 2-8°C (36-46°F). Scotland and northern England are coldest, often dropping to 0°C or below, with occasional snow. London rarely drops below freezing for long.

Which UK city is warmest?

London is generally the warmest UK city, especially in summer. Its urban heat effect can add 1-2°C to surrounding areas. In winter, the south-west (Cornwall, Devon) tends to be mildest.
