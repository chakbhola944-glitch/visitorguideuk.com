How Much Do UK Hotels Cost in 2026?

Hotel prices in the UK depend mostly on three things: the city, the time of year and the type of hotel. As a rough guide, a mid-range room costs about £105 to £175 per night, and London sits at the top of that range. The estimator above uses typical mid-season rates for 13 cities, then adjusts them for low and high season. These are estimates, not live prices, so use them to build your budget and then check real rates for your exact dates before you book.

Accommodation is usually the biggest single cost of a UK trip, so it pays to understand what drives the price. This guide explains what you can expect to pay in each city, what you get at each level, how the seasons change the cost, and how to avoid paying more than you need to.

Hotel prices by city

London. By far the most expensive city. Our estimate is about £95 per night for a budget room, £175 for mid-range and £350 for luxury. Prices climb in summer and around Christmas. Where you stay matters as much as when: rooms near a tube station in zones 2 or 3 are often much cheaper than the West End. Our London travel guide covers the main areas to stay.

Edinburgh. About £80 budget, £150 mid-range and £300 luxury. Demand is strong all year and rises sharply in August, when the festivals fill the city. Book early for summer, and read the note on the visitor levy below. See our Edinburgh guide for the best neighbourhoods.

Bath and York. Both are small, popular cities where hotels fill quickly. Bath is about £85, £155 and £290, while York is about £75, £135 and £240. Weekends are busier and more expensive than weekdays in both. Rooms inside the historic centre cost more, and a short walk or bus ride out often saves money. See our guides to Bath and York.

Oxford. About £80, £145 and £270. Graduation weeks and summer term events push prices up, and the city is popular as a day trip from London, so staying overnight midweek can be good value. Read more in our Oxford guide.

Manchester, Liverpool and Birmingham. Good value for large cities. Mid-range rooms cost about £110 to £120 per night. These are business cities, so weekday prices can be higher than weekend prices, and big football matches or conferences can raise rates for a night or two. See the guides for Manchester, Liverpool and Birmingham.

Glasgow, Leeds, Newcastle, Cardiff and Bristol. These are usually the cheapest places to stay. Budget rooms start around £55 to £65 and mid-range rooms around £105 to £120. Newcastle is the lowest in our estimates, while Bristol is slightly higher at about £65 budget and £120 mid-range. They are good bases for a UK trip on a tight budget.

Budget, mid-range and luxury: what you get

  • Budget (Premier Inn, Travelodge, ibis, hostels): clean, simple rooms, often a short distance from the centre. Breakfast is normally extra. This is the best choice if you will spend most of your day out.
  • Mid-range (Hilton Garden Inn, Hotel Indigo, boutique hotels and B&Bs): better locations, more space and usually a restaurant or breakfast option on site. This is the sweet spot for most couples and families.
  • Luxury (five-star and country-house hotels): prime locations, spas, fine dining and a high level of service. In London and Edinburgh these can easily pass £300 per night.

Our estimator shows all three tiers side by side for the city, nights and season you choose, so you can see how much more a step up in comfort really costs.

How the season changes the price

The estimator treats January, February and November as low season, about 25% cheaper than mid-season. March to June and September to October are mid-season. July, August and December are high season, about 35% more expensive. For example, a London budget room of £95 in mid-season works out at about £71 in low season and £128 in high season.

These are averages. Big events can push prices far above the seasonal figure for a few days: the Edinburgh Fringe in August, major concerts and festivals, graduation weeks in university cities, and large matches in Manchester, Liverpool and Cardiff. If your dates match a major event, treat the high-season figure as a minimum and book as early as you can.

Example stay costs

  • 3 nights in London, mid-range, mid-season: 3 × £175 = about £525 for one room.
  • Weekend in York (2 nights), mid-range: 2 × £135 = about £270.
  • 7 nights in Manchester, budget: 7 × £65 = about £455.
  • 4 nights in Edinburgh, mid-range: 4 × £150 = £600, plus the visitor levy, so roughly £630.
  • London family trip, 2 rooms for 3 nights, budget, high season: 2 × 3 × £128 = about £768.

Edinburgh's visitor levy

Since 24 July 2026, Edinburgh charges a 5% visitor levy on paid overnight accommodation, capped at the first five consecutive nights of a stay. It is worked out on the room cost only. On a £150 room, that is £7.50 per night, or £37.50 for five nights. Our estimator does not add the levy automatically, so include it when you budget for Edinburgh. Check the City of Edinburgh Council website for the latest rules.

Hotel or apartment?

For one or two people, a hotel is usually the simplest and often the cheapest option, especially a budget chain. For families or groups of four or more, a self-catering apartment can work out cheaper per person on stays of three nights or more, because you can cook some meals and share one booking. Hotels tend to win for short city breaks, where you want a central location, a daily clean and flexible check-in. Apartments suit longer stays, larger groups and travellers who want a kitchen and washing machine.

Extra costs to check before you book

  • Breakfast: budget chains usually charge extra, typically around £8 to £15 per person per morning.
  • Parking: city-centre hotels often charge for it, sometimes a lot. Use our car parking cost calculator to estimate it.
  • Cancellation terms: a flexible rate costs a little more but protects you if your plans change.
  • Extra guests: most rooms are priced for up to two adults, and children or a third adult may add a supplement.
  • Taxes and fees: check whether the price shown includes VAT and any local charges.

How to pay less for a UK hotel

  1. Book 4 to 8 weeks ahead for normal dates, and 2 to 3 months ahead for summer, Christmas and big events.
  2. Travel from January to early March, when prices are usually lowest.
  3. Choose the right days. In business cities like London, Manchester and Birmingham, weekends are often cheaper. In tourist cities like Bath, York and Edinburgh, midweek is cheaper.
  4. Stay near a station, not in the centre. A short train or tube ride can save a lot. Compare fares with our train ticket price calculator.
  5. Compare several sites and the hotel's own website, which sometimes has a better rate or free extras.
  6. Choose a flexible rate if prices may drop, then rebook if you find a cheaper deal before the cancellation deadline.
  7. Skip the hotel breakfast at budget chains if a nearby café is cheaper.

Common booking mistakes

  • Booking too late for peak dates. The cheapest rooms go first, and prices rarely fall at the last minute.
  • Ignoring the location. A cheap room far from the centre can cost more in transport and time than you save.
  • Forgetting extras. Parking, breakfast and levies can add 10% to 20% to the headline price.
  • Not checking events. A concert or festival can double the price of one night, and a one-day shift in your dates may avoid it.
  • Choosing a non-refundable rate too early. It saves a little money but gives you no flexibility if your plans change.

Sample budgets for a UK trip

Budget weekend in Manchester or Liverpool: two nights in a budget hotel at about £60 to £65 comes to around £120 to £130 for a room. Mid-range city break in York or Bath: two or three nights at about £135 to £155 comes to £270 to £465. A week in Edinburgh, mid-range: 7 × £150 = £1,050, plus the levy on the first five nights, about £37.50, so around £1,090. A week in London, mid-range: 7 × £175 = £1,225 for one room.

Frequently Asked Questions

💷 How much does a hotel cost in London per night?

In London, expect to pay around £70-£120 per night for a budget hotel, £130-£220 for mid-range, and £250-£500+ for luxury. Prices rise significantly in summer and around Christmas.

🏴 How much does a hotel cost in Edinburgh per night?

Edinburgh hotels typically cost £60-£100 for budget, £110-£200 for mid-range, and £220-£450+ for luxury. August (Fringe Festival) sees prices double or triple.

🇬🇧 Are hotels cheaper outside London?

Yes — significantly. Manchester, Liverpool, Birmingham, Glasgow and Cardiff are typically 30-50% cheaper than London for equivalent accommodation. Cities like York and Bath sit in between.

📅 What is the cheapest time to book a UK hotel?

Book 4-8 weeks ahead for the best prices, and travel outside school holidays and peak summer. January, February and early March are the cheapest months. Avoid booking same-day unless there's a last-minute deal.

⭐ What is the difference between budget, mid-range and luxury hotels in the UK?

Budget: Premier Inn, Travelodge, ibis, hostels — clean, basic, £50-£100. Mid-range: Hilton Garden Inn, Hotel Indigo, boutique B&Bs — £100-£200. Luxury: The Ritz, Claridge's, Gleneagles — £250-£1000+ with fine dining and spas.

📊 How much does a 7-night UK hotel stay cost?

For a mid-range 7-night stay: around £1,050-£1,540 in London, or £630-£1,050 in other UK cities. Budget travellers can keep it under £500; luxury travellers should budget £2,000+.

🍳 Do UK hotel prices include breakfast?

Not always. Mid-range and luxury hotels often include breakfast, but budget chains like Premier Inn and Travelodge usually charge extra (£8-£15 per person). Always check the room rate details before booking.

🔥 When is the most expensive time to stay in a UK hotel?

Peak periods: July-August (school holidays), Christmas and New Year, Easter weekend, and major events (Wimbledon, Edinburgh Fringe, London Marathon, big football matches). Prices can double or triple during these times.

💰 What is the average hotel price in the UK?

The average UK hotel room costs around £95-£120 per night outside London and £150-£200 in London. Budget chains start around £50, while luxury hotels can exceed £500 per night.

🏨 How much is a Premier Inn per night?

Premier Inn rooms typically cost £50-£90 per night outside London and £90-£150 in London. Prices are higher on weekends, bank holidays and during major events.

🏙️ What is the cheapest city for hotels in the UK?

Newcastle, Leeds, Liverpool and Cardiff are among the cheapest UK cities for hotels, with budget rooms often available for £45-£65 per night. Glasgow is also affordable compared to Edinburgh.

⏰ How far in advance should I book a UK hotel?

For the best prices, book 4-8 weeks ahead for standard dates, and 2-3 months ahead for peak periods like summer, Christmas and major events. Same-day bookings rarely save money in the UK.

📆 Are UK hotels cheaper on weekdays or weekends?

In business cities like London, Manchester and Birmingham, weekend rates are often lower. In tourist cities like Bath, York and Edinburgh, weekends are usually more expensive than weekdays.

🏠 Is it cheaper to stay in a hotel or Airbnb in the UK?

It depends. For 1-2 people, budget hotels are often cheaper. For families or groups of 4+, Airbnb and self-catering apartments usually work out cheaper per person, especially for stays of 3+ nights.

👥 Do UK hotels charge per person or per room?

Most UK hotels charge per room, not per person. The room rate usually covers up to 2 adults. Extra guests (children or additional adults) may incur a small supplement.
