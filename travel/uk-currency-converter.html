UK Currency: What Tourists Need to Know

The official currency of the United Kingdom is the British Pound Sterling (£, GBP). It's one of the oldest and most traded currencies in the world. If you're visiting the UK, you'll need to convert your money to pounds — and where you do the conversion matters a lot.

🇬🇧 What is the British Pound?

The British Pound (£) is divided into 100 pence (p). Coins come in 1p, 2p, 5p, 10p, 20p, 50p, £1 and £2. Notes come in £5, £10, £20 and £50. Scotland and Northern Ireland issue their own banknotes, which are valid throughout the UK but sometimes refused outside their home region.

💰 Money-Saving Tips for Your UK Trip

  • Use a fee-free travel card — Wise, Revolut, Monzo or Starling use near mid-market rates and no foreign transaction fees.
  • Avoid airport exchanges — they're consistently the worst value, sometimes 12% above the mid-market rate.
  • Withdraw cash from ATMs in the UK — once you arrive, using your travel card at an ATM usually gets the best rate.
  • Pay by card, not cash — the UK is heavily card-based. Contactless is accepted almost everywhere.
  • Bring only a small amount of cash — £50-£100 is enough for most trips. You'll need it for a few small shops, public toilets and market stalls.
  • Don't exchange at the last minute — plan ahead and get a travel card before you leave home.

💳 How much cash do you really need in the UK?

The UK runs almost entirely on contactless card payments. You can pay by card for a coffee, a bus ticket, a museum entry, or even a market stall. Cash is only needed for:

  • Some small independent shops
  • Pay-to-use public toilets
  • Some market stalls that offer discounts for cash
  • Emergency backup if a card machine is down

For most trips, £50-£100 in cash is more than enough. Everything else can be paid by card.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is £100 in US dollars?

At current exchange rates, £100 is approximately $130 USD. However, exchange rates change daily, so always check our live converter for the most up-to-date figure.

How much is £100 in euros?

At current exchange rates, £100 is approximately €118 EUR. Actual exchange rates vary by provider — banks and exchange bureaux typically charge 2-5% above the mid-market rate.

What is the best way to exchange money for a UK trip?

The best value is usually a fee-free travel card like Wise, Revolut, Monzo or Starling, which use near mid-market rates. Avoid airport currency exchange counters, which typically charge 8-12% above the mid-market rate.

Is it cheaper to exchange money in the UK or my home country?

It depends. In most cases, exchanging money in your home country before you travel gives better rates than exchanging at UK airports. However, using a fee-free travel card for purchases is usually cheaper than either option.

How much cash should I bring for a UK trip?

For most trips, £50-£100 in cash is enough. The UK is heavily card-based — contactless payment is accepted almost everywhere. You only need cash for a few small shops, public toilets, and market stalls.

What is the mid-market exchange rate?

The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the buy and sell prices of two currencies. It's the fairest rate you can get, and it's what you see on Google. Banks and exchange bureaux typically add a markup of 2-12% on top of this rate.

Are exchange rates better on weekdays or weekends?

Exchange rates fluctuate constantly based on global markets, and the forex market is closed on weekends. Weekday rates are generally more stable, but no single day is consistently better than another.
