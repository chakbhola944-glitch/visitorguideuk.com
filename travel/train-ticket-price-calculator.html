How Train Fares Work in the UK

1

Advance Tickets

Cheapest fares, released 12 weeks before travel. Only valid on your specific train. Book 4-12 weeks ahead for the best prices.

2

Off-Peak Tickets

Flexible but only valid outside peak hours (typically before 6:30am, between 9:30am-4pm, and after 7pm). Good for flexible travel.

3

Anytime Tickets

Most expensive but fully flexible - valid on any train. Only worth buying if you genuinely need flexibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this UK train ticket price calculator free?

Yes, completely free. No sign-up, no email, no payment. Just pick two cities and your travel details to get an instant price comparison.

Are these live train ticket prices?

No. These are typical 2026 estimates based on average prices for each fare type. Actual prices vary by train operator, time of day, how far ahead you book, and availability. Always check Trainline, National Rail or the operator's website for live prices.

How much is a train ticket from London to Edinburgh?

As a rough guide: Advance single from £35-£60 booked 4+ weeks ahead, Off-Peak from £60-£90, Anytime from £110-£180. Prices rise significantly if you book last minute.

What's the cheapest way to buy UK train tickets?

Buy Advance tickets directly from the train operator or Trainline 4-12 weeks before travel. These are the cheapest fares but come with restrictions - they're only valid on the specific train you booked.

What is the difference between Advance, Off-Peak and Anytime tickets?

Advance tickets are cheapest but only valid on a specific train. Off-Peak tickets are flexible but only valid outside peak hours. Anytime tickets are the most expensive but can be used on any train.

Do Railcards really save money on UK trains?

Yes. A Railcard typically costs £30-£100 per year and gives 1/3 off most train fares. If you'll spend more than £90-£300 on train tickets in a year, a Railcard pays for itself.

What are the main UK Railcards?

The main ones are: 16-25 Railcard (£35/yr), 26-30 Railcard (£30/yr), Two Together (£30/yr, for 2 adults travelling together), Family & Friends (£30/yr), Senior Railcard (£30/yr for 60+), and Network Railcard (South East England).

Is split ticketing worth it in the UK?

On some long routes, yes. Split ticketing means buying separate tickets for different legs of the same journey - often cheaper than a single through-fare. Savings are usually 20-50% on longer routes but you must travel on the specific trains you booked.
