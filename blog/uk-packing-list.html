UK Packing List 2026: What to Pack for Every Season

Updated 8 October 20269 min read

Quick Answer

The UK's weather is famously changeable — it can rain, shine and cool down in a single day. The safest approach is layers: a t-shirt, a light jumper and a waterproof jacket. Packing for the UK is trickier than it looks. The weather forecasts are unreliable, "summer" can mean 15°C and drizzle, and a sunny morning can turn into a rainy afternoon. This guide breaks down exactly what to pack for each season — based on what UK locals actually wear and what visitors consistently forget.

The Golden Rule: Layers, Not Bulk

Don't pack for a specific forecast — pack for variability. Three layers (t-shirt, jumper, waterproof jacket) will handle nearly every UK day from March to October. You'll add a proper winter coat for December to February.

The goal is to be able to add or remove a layer as the day changes, without having to go back to your hotel.

Season-by-Season Packing Guide

🌱 Spring (March–May)

4-17°C / 39-63°F

Spring is one of the most pleasant times to visit — mild temperatures, longer days and fewer crowds than summer. But it's also the season of "April showers" — short bursts of rain that can appear out of nowhere. Pack for changeable weather.

  • Layered clothing (t-shirt + jumper + jacket)
  • Light waterproof rain jacket
  • Compact umbrella
  • Waterproof walking shoes
  • One warm jumper for cool evenings
  • Jeans or comfortable trousers
  • Sunglasses (sunny days are common)
  • A scarf for early March

☀️ Summer (June–August)

12-25°C / 54-77°F

Don't be fooled by "summer" — UK summer temperatures rarely exceed 25°C (77°F), and even July can deliver cool, wet days. Pack for warm weather, but bring a rain layer and one warm jumper.

  • Light layers (t-shirt, light jumper)
  • A light rain jacket, just in case
  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Sunglasses and a sun hat
  • A light jumper for evenings
  • Shorts and t-shirts for warm days
  • Sunscreen (SPF 30+)
  • One warmer layer for the north

🍂 Autumn (September–November)

5-19°C / 41-66°F

September is arguably the best month to visit the UK — warm, less crowded and often sunny. By November, rain becomes more frequent and days shorten significantly. Pack for cooler weather with a good waterproof layer.

  • Waterproof jacket
  • Layered clothing
  • Scarf
  • Sturdy waterproof shoes
  • Compact umbrella
  • A warm jumper or fleece
  • Long trousers or jeans
  • Light gloves for late November

❄️ Winter (December–February)

2-8°C / 36-46°F

UK winter is cold, wet and dark by 4pm. It rarely drops below freezing for long, but the damp cold feels colder than dry cold. Pack seriously for warmth — you'll be outside a lot even in winter, and heating indoors varies.

  • Warm insulated winter coat
  • Thermal base layers (top and bottom)
  • Wool hat, gloves and scarf
  • Waterproof, insulated boots
  • Compact umbrella
  • Warm socks (multiple pairs)
  • Fleece or thick jumper
  • Waterproof gloves

Essentials for Every UK Trip

Whatever the season, these are the items experienced UK travellers always bring:

📄

Documents

  • Passport (check validity)
  • Travel insurance
  • UK ETA / visa confirmation
  • Printed booking confirmations
🔌

Tech & Power

  • UK plug adapter (Type G)
  • Phone charger
  • Portable power bank
  • Headphones for trains
💊

Health & Personal

  • Any prescription medication
  • Basic pain relief (paracetamol)
  • Plasters / blister care
  • Rehydration sachets
💳

Money & Payments

  • Contactless card (Visa/Mastercard)
  • Small amount of cash (£50-£100)
  • Second card as backup
  • Fee-free travel card (Wise, Revolut)
🎒

Day Bag

  • Comfortable backpack (20-25L)
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Compact umbrella
  • Crossbody bag for valuables
👟

Footwear

  • Comfortable walking shoes
  • Waterproof trainers or boots
  • One smarter pair for dinners
  • Blister plasters for day one

What NOT to Pack for the UK

Save space and skip these — you won't need them:

  • Heavy winter gear in summer — even Scotland is rarely below 10°C in July.
  • Too many shoes — two pairs is usually enough; three max.
  • A hairdryer — most UK hotels and B&Bs provide one.
  • Large amounts of cash — the UK is nearly cashless. £50-£100 is plenty as backup.
  • Beach towels — unless you're specifically going to a UK beach resort, hotels provide them.
  • Formal wear — unless you have a specific event, "smart casual" covers almost every UK restaurant.

Pro Packing Tips

  • Pack one outfit in your carry-on. UK weather delays are rare, but lost luggage happens — having one change of clothes saves the first day.
  • Use packing cubes. Especially helpful if you're visiting multiple cities on one trip and repacking often.
  • Bring a foldable tote bag. Useful for shopping, laundry, or an extra layer you shed during the day.
  • Choose the right suitcase size. For a 5-7 day trip, one carry-on (40-55L) plus a daypack works well. UK trains, taxis and small B&Bs are easier to navigate with lighter luggage.
  • Do laundry once mid-trip. UK self-service laundrettes are common and cost £4-£8 per load. This lets you pack lighter.
  • Wear your bulkiest items on the plane. Winter coat, boots and jeans don't need to take up suitcase space.
