Cheapest Day to Travel by Train in the UK (2026 Guide)

UK Train Travel9 min read

Updated October 2026 · Fares verified quarterly

If you're flexible about when you travel, you can save 40-70% on UK train fares just by picking the right day. Here's the answer, straight — plus the times, booking windows and tricks that matter most.

The Short Answer: Tuesday, Wednesday & Saturday

Across the UK rail network, the cheapest days to travel are generally Tuesday, Wednesday and Saturday. Fridays and Sundays are the most expensive because of weekend and commuter demand. Here's how the week breaks down:

Day Relative Price Why
MondayHighCommuter demand — expensive morning and evening
TuesdayLowestQuietest day on most routes — cheapest Advance fares
WednesdayLowestSame as Tuesday — mid-week sweet spot
ThursdayLowSlightly pricier than Tue/Wed but still good
FridayHighWeekend commuters + leisure travellers — very expensive
SaturdayLowUsually Off-Peak all day — good value
SundayMid-HighOften Off-Peak but engineering works reduce capacity
£40–£70
Typical saving on a London–Edinburgh return
By travelling Tuesday or Wednesday instead of Friday, you can save £40-£70 on the same journey.

Best Times of Day to Travel

The day matters, but so does the time. On weekdays, fares are split into Peak and Off-Peak. Off-Peak is significantly cheaper.

  • Off-Peak morning: after 9:30am (sometimes 9:00am on some routes)
  • Off-Peak afternoon: between 9:30am and 4pm
  • Off-Peak evening: after 7pm
  • Weekends: Off-Peak all day (Saturday and Sunday)
  • Bank holidays: usually Off-Peak all day
Peak travel is 6:30am–9:30am and 4pm–7pm on weekdays. Avoid these windows if you can — the same journey can cost 2-3 times more.

The Real Money-Saver: Advance Tickets

Booking an Advance ticket 4-12 weeks ahead is by far the biggest saving you can make. Advance tickets are released 12 weeks before travel and are only valid on the specific train you book.

Here's how the prices compare on a typical London–Manchester route:

Ticket Type Typical Price When to Book
Advance Single£25–£454-12 weeks ahead
Off-Peak Single£60–£85Anytime
Anytime Single£120–£180On the day

Bottom line: Advance tickets are usually 60-70% cheaper than Anytime tickets on the same route. Book them as soon as they're released.

Use a Railcard

A Railcard costs £30-£100 per year and gives 1/3 off most train fares. If you'll spend more than £90-£300 on train tickets in a year, a Railcard pays for itself.

  • 16-25 Railcard — £35/yr for full-time students and under-25s
  • 26-30 Railcard — £30/yr for 26-30 year olds
  • Two Together Railcard — £30/yr for 2 adults travelling together
  • Family & Friends Railcard — £30/yr for 1-4 adults + up to 4 children
  • Senior Railcard — £30/yr for 60+
  • Network Railcard — £30/yr for South East England

Railcards can be used with Advance tickets, and the discount stacks with Off-Peak fares. For a family of four travelling London–Edinburgh, the Family & Friends Railcard can save £80+ on a single journey.

Try Split Ticketing

Split ticketing means buying separate tickets for different legs of the same journey — often cheaper than a single through-fare. On long routes, savings can be 20-50%.

For example, London to Edinburgh might be cheaper as London–York + York–Edinburgh (two separate tickets) rather than one London–Edinburgh ticket. You stay on the same train — you just have multiple tickets.

Warning: With split ticketing you must travel on the specific trains you booked. If you miss a connection, you'll need to buy a new ticket for the rest of the journey.

When to Book: The 12-Week Rule

Advance tickets are released exactly 12 weeks before travel. This is when the cheapest fares appear — and when the widest choice is available.

  • 12 weeks ahead: Cheapest Advance tickets released
  • 8 weeks ahead: Good Advance tickets still available
  • 4 weeks ahead: Advance tickets still cheaper than Off-Peak, but limited
  • 1 week ahead: Most Advance tickets sold out; Off-Peak or Anytime only
  • On the day: Anytime tickets only — the most expensive option

Set a calendar reminder 12 weeks before your trip. Booking on release day is the single biggest thing you can do to save money on UK train tickets.
