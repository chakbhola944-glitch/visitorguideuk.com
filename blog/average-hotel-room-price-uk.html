Average Hotel Room Price in the UK (2026)

What will you actually pay for a hotel room in the UK? We've broken down the latest 2026 data for London, Edinburgh, and regional cities — plus how to find cheaper rooms with tools like Premier Inn's price finder.

By Visitor Guide UK Updated October 2026

Hotel prices in the UK vary wildly depending on where you go, when you travel, and how far in advance you book. A room in central London can cost three times as much as a similar room in Manchester or Cardiff.

To help you budget realistically, we've gathered the latest 2026 data from industry trackers like RSM UK and HotelsCombined, and broken it down by city and season.

The National Average: What the Data Says

According to RSM UK's hotel tracker, the average daily rate (ADR) for occupied hotel rooms across the UK was £156.38 in August 2026, up from £152.80 the previous year [citation:1][citation:10]. This figure represents what hotels actually charged per room, per night, on average.

However, other sources report different numbers depending on methodology. SiteMinder's mid-year report put the UK summer ADR at £254, though this likely reflects a different sample of hotels and booking patterns [citation:6]. The key takeaway: expect to pay somewhere between £150 and £250 per night for a standard UK hotel room, depending on location and season.

"The UK average daily rate was £156.38 in August 2026, but London was significantly higher at £214.90." — RSM UK

London vs the Rest of the UK

London is in a league of its own. In August 2026, the capital's average daily rate was £214.90, compared to the UK-wide average of £156.38 [citation:1]. During peak summer months (July), London rates jumped to £274.40 [citation:2].

Outside London, prices drop considerably. Regional cities like Manchester, Birmingham, and Cardiff typically see average rates in the £100–£150 range, depending on events and seasonality.

Edinburgh: The Festival Effect

Edinburgh is a special case. HotelsCombined data shows an average of around £196 per night, but prices rise sharply in July and August during the Edinburgh Festival Fringe [citation:13]. Budget rooms can be found from around £80 outside peak periods, but during the festival, even basic rooms can exceed £200.

Premier Inn Prices: What to Expect

Premier Inn is the UK's largest budget hotel chain, and it's a useful benchmark for what "affordable" looks like. Prices vary by location and demand, but here are some real examples from 2026:

  • Premier Inn Exeter City Centre: Rooms from £87 per night (room only) [citation:11].
  • Premier Inn London Ilford (Newbury Park): Prices ranged from $58 to $131 (roughly £45–£100) depending on the night, based on Trivago's 30-day average .
  • Premier Inn London Bromley: Prices ranged from €74 to €222 (roughly £60–£190) depending on the date .

The pattern is clear: Premier Inn outside London can be very affordable (£60–£100), while London locations are cheaper on weekdays and Sundays but spike on Friday and Saturday nights.

If you want to check prices for specific dates, Premier Inn's own website has a price finder tool that lets you search by city and date. We'd recommend using it alongside our Hotel Cost Estimator to compare chains and cities.

How Expensive Is a Hotel in the UK? (By Season)

Prices aren't flat across the year. Based on RSM UK's monthly data:

  • January–February: Cheapest months. UK average around £130–£140. London around £180–£190.
  • May–June: Prices rise. UK average around £156–£160.
  • July–August: Peak season. UK average £180+; London £270+.
  • September–October: Prices begin to fall. UK average around £150–£160.
  • November–December: Christmas markets push prices up, especially in cities like Edinburgh, York, and Bath.

How to Find Cheaper Hotel Rooms

If the average prices above feel high, here are practical ways to reduce your bill:

  • Book in advance: Premier Inn and Travelodge often release rooms at lower "Saver" rates months ahead.
  • Avoid Friday and Saturday nights: Weekend prices can be 50% higher than midweek in many UK cities.
  • Stay outside the city centre: A 10-minute bus or train ride can save £30–£50 per night.
  • Use our tools: Estimate your total accommodation cost with the Hotel Cost Estimator and see how it fits into your overall budget with the Trip Cost Calculator.

The Bottom Line

The average UK hotel room price in 2026 is roughly £150–£160 per night, but this figure hides enormous regional variation. London averages over £210, while regional cities and budget chains can be £60–£100. The key to getting a good deal is booking early, avoiding peak weekends, and knowing where to look.
