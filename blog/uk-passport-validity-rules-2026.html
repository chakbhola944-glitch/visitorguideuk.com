UK Passport Validity Rules 2026: The 10-Year Rule That's Catching Out Thousands

7 October 20268 min read

Quick Answer

If your UK passport was issued more than 10 years ago, it may be treated as expired for EU travel — even if it says it's valid until 2027 or 2028. Countries like the USA, Australia and much of Asia require at least 6 months of validity. Before you book any trip, check your passport's issue date, not just its expiry date.

Every day, hundreds of British travellers are turned away at airport check-in because of a passport rule most people have never heard of. It's called the 10-year rule, and it's costing travellers thousands of pounds in cancelled holidays. Here's exactly what it is, who it affects, and how to check if your passport is safe.

What Is the 10-Year Rule?

Under Schengen rules, your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before your date of entry into the EU. It's not about when your passport expires — it's about when it was issued.

Here's the problem. If you renewed your UK passport before September 2018, the UK Passport Office would add up to 9 months of remaining validity from your old passport onto your new one. That was a helpful policy at the time — but it means your new passport can show a valid-until date that is more than 10 years after its issue date.

In the EU's eyes, that passport is expired. You cannot enter.

⚠️ A Real Example

Your passport was issued on 1 June 2016 and expires on 1 December 2026. It looks valid for another 2 months. But Schengen countries only recognise the first 10 years from the issue date — so after 1 June 2026, this passport no longer meets EU entry requirements. You will be denied boarding.

Who Does This Affect?

Anyone whose passport was renewed before September 2018 may have extra months from their old passport added. That's millions of UK passports still in circulation. If yours was renewed after September 2018, the extra months are no longer added, so you're likely safe.

To be certain, look at the Issue Date printed on your passport's photo page.

What About Other Countries?

The 10-year rule applies to the EU and Schengen area. But other countries have their own rules — most commonly, they require at least 6 months of validity beyond your planned departure date. Here's a quick guide:

Destination Rule
EU / Schengen Passport must be issued less than 10 years before entry. Must be valid for 3 months after planned departure.
Ireland No 10-year rule. Standard validity is enough.
USA Passport must be valid for the duration of your stay. 6 months is recommended.
Australia, China, Thailand, UAE, India, Indonesia, Vietnam, Kenya, Brazil 6 months validity beyond planned departure date.
Canada Varies — check GOV.UK for current requirements.

How to Check If Your Passport Is Safe

✓ Your 4-Step Check

  1. Find the Issue Date on your passport photo page (look for "Date of issue").
  2. Add 10 years to that date. If your planned entry date is later, your passport fails EU rules.
  3. Check the expiry date. It must be at least 3 months after your planned return from the EU.
  4. Check your specific destination on GOV.UK Foreign Travel Advice. Rules vary country by country.

What About the EU's New EES System?

The EU has introduced the Entry/Exit System (EES), which registers biometric data — fingerprints and a photo — for non-EU travellers entering the Schengen area. UK citizens will need to register on their first entry.

The registration is valid for 3 years. It does not replace the need for a valid passport — you still need to meet the 10-year rule and the 3-month rule.

What If You're Refused Boarding?

If you're turned away at check-in because of your passport, you are not entitled to compensation. It's the passenger's responsibility to meet the destination country's entry requirements — not the airline's.

Your best option is to try to get an emergency passport appointment at an HMPO office, though this is unlikely to save a same-day flight. Travel insurance typically does not cover passport-related denial of boarding.

To avoid this situation: check your passport the day you book your trip — not the day before you fly. Give yourself at least 12 weeks of buffer if you're travelling in summer.

Do You Need a UK ETA or Visa Instead?

If you're coming to the UK rather than leaving it, the rules are different. Most visitors now need a UK ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) — but the ETA is separate from your passport and doesn't replace passport validity requirements.
