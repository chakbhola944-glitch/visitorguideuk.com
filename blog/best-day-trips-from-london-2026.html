Best Day Trips from London 2026: 12 Unmissable Escapes

From royal castles to ancient stone circles, here are the best day trips from London — with train times, costs, and practical tips for each destination.

By Visitor Guide UK Updated November 2026

London is a fantastic city, but sometimes you need a break from the crowds. The good news? Some of the UK's most iconic destinations are just a short train ride away. Whether you're into history, nature, or simply a change of scenery, there's a perfect day trip waiting for you.

Here are the 12 best day trips from London, all doable in a single day without a car.

1. Windsor Castle

Travel time: 30 minutes from London Paddington (or 1 hour from Waterloo).

Windsor Castle is the oldest and largest inhabited castle in the world, and it's the Queen's preferred weekend home. The State Apartments are breathtaking, and if you're lucky, you might catch the Changing of the Guard. The town of Windsor itself is charming, with plenty of pubs and cafes for lunch.

Why go: A perfect blend of royal history and easy access.

2. Oxford

Travel time: 50-60 minutes from London Paddington or Marylebone.

Oxford is the city of dreaming spires, home to the oldest university in the English-speaking world. Visit the Bodleian Library, Christ Church College (recognisable from Harry Potter), and the Radcliffe Camera. It's also a great place to simply wander — the architecture is stunning.

Why go: History, architecture, and a quintessentially English atmosphere.

3. Bath

Travel time: 80 minutes from London Paddington.

Bath is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, famous for its Roman baths and stunning Georgian architecture. The Roman Baths are a must-see, and the city's honey-coloured stone makes it one of the most beautiful in England. Don't miss the Royal Crescent and Pulteney Bridge.

Why go: Unique history and some of the finest architecture in the UK.

4. Stonehenge

Travel time: 1.5 hours to Salisbury by train, then 30 minutes by bus.

Stonehenge is one of the world's most famous prehistoric monuments. The easiest way to visit is by organised tour from London (around £60-£90 including entry). If you go independently, you can combine it with a visit to Salisbury Cathedral, which has the tallest spire in the UK.

Why go: A once-in-a-lifetime experience.

5. Cambridge

Travel time: 50 minutes from London King's Cross.

Cambridge is Oxford's rival, and arguably even more picturesque. Punting on the River Cam is a must-do, and the colleges — particularly King's College Chapel — are stunning. It's a smaller, more intimate city than Oxford, and easy to explore on foot.

Why go: A beautiful, compact city with a unique atmosphere.

6. The Cotswolds

Travel time: 1.5 hours to Moreton-in-Marsh by train.

The Cotswolds is an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, characterised by rolling hills, honey-coloured villages, and cosy pubs. The easiest way to explore is by car, but you can also base yourself in a village like Bourton-on-the-Water and explore on foot.

Why go: Quintessential English countryside at its finest.

"The best day trips from London are the ones where you step off the train and instantly feel like you're in a different world."

7. Brighton

Travel time: 1 hour from London Victoria.

Brighton is London's seaside escape. It's known for its pier, its pebble beach, and its vibrant LGBTQ+ scene. The Royal Pavilion is a must-see — an extraordinary piece of Indian-inspired architecture. The Lanes, a maze of narrow streets, are perfect for shopping and eating.

Why go: A fun, lively seaside town with a unique character.

8. Canterbury

Travel time: 1 hour from London St Pancras.

Canterbury is the spiritual home of the Church of England, and its cathedral is one of the most important in the country. The city itself is a charming mix of medieval streets and modern shops. It's also a great base for exploring the Kent countryside.

Why go: A beautiful cathedral city with a rich history.

9. Stratford-upon-Avon

Travel time: 2 hours from London Marylebone.

Stratford-upon-Avon is the birthplace of William Shakespeare, and it's a lovely place to explore. You can visit Shakespeare's Birthplace, Anne Hathaway's Cottage, and the Royal Shakespeare Theatre. The town itself is full of historic buildings and charming streets.

Why go: A pilgrimage for literature lovers.

10. York

Travel time: 2 hours from London King's Cross.

York is one of the most beautiful cities in England, with its medieval walls, stunning Minster, and the famous Shambles. It's a longer day trip, but absolutely worth it. If you have time, stay overnight — there's so much to see.

Why go: One of the UK's most historic and photogenic cities.

11. The Seven Sisters

Travel time: 1.5 hours to Seaford by train.

The Seven Sisters are a series of chalk cliffs on the Sussex coast, and they're one of the most dramatic landscapes in southern England. The walk from Seaford to Eastbourne is one of the best coastal walks in the UK. It's a full day out, but the views are unforgettable.

Why go: Stunning coastal scenery and a great walk.

12. Hampton Court Palace

Travel time: 35 minutes from London Waterloo.

Hampton Court Palace was the home of Henry VIII, and it's one of the most spectacular palaces in England. The gardens are beautiful, and the palace itself is full of fascinating history. It's an easy half-day trip, and you can combine it with a walk along the River Thames.

Why go: Royal history at its most accessible.
