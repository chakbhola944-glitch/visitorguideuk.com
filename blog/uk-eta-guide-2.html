UK ETA Guide: Who Needs It, What It Costs & How to Apply

Last Updated: September 2026

A plain-English, fact-checked guide to the UK's Electronic Travel Authorisation, based directly on official Home Office and GOV.UK information — not outdated videos or third-party guesswork.

Quick Answer

Since 25 February 2026, almost every visa-exempt visitor to the UK — including citizens of the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and all EU/EEA countries — needs an approved Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) before boarding any transport to the UK. It costs £20, is applied for online or via the official UK ETA app, is usually approved within minutes, and remains valid for two years (or until your passport expires) for multiple visits of up to six months each.

The Basics

What Is a UK ETA?

An Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) is a digital permission to travel to the UK — it is not a visa, and it does not by itself guarantee entry. It confirms that the Home Office has checked your details in advance and cleared you to board a flight, ferry, train or other transport to the UK. A Border Force officer still makes the final decision on your entry when you arrive, though many eligible travellers can now use eGates.

The scheme is similar in concept to the United States' ESTA or Canada's eTA, and it launched in October 2023, rolling out in phases to different nationalities before becoming fully mandatory for all visa-exempt visitors on 25 February 2026.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Eligibility

Who Needs a UK ETA?

You need an ETA if you are a visa-exempt visitor — meaning you don't currently need a visa for a short UK visit — and you don't already hold UK immigration status. This covers a wide range of nationalities, including:

  • The United States
  • Canada
  • Australia and New Zealand
  • All European Union and EEA member states (since 2 April 2025)
  • Japan, South Korea, Singapore and most other visa-exempt nationalities

In total, around 85 nationalities require an ETA. Since 25 February 2026, this has applied without exception — eligible visitors without an approved ETA cannot board transport to the UK and cannot legally travel.

Because eligibility depends on the exact nationality shown on your passport and your specific circumstances, the official GOV.UK checker is always the most reliable way to confirm whether you personally need one — this guide covers the general rules, but edge cases exist.

Exemptions

Who Is Exempt From Needing an ETA?

  • British and Irish citizens — including dual citizens, who should travel on a British or Irish passport (or a Certificate of Entitlement).
  • Visa nationals — if you already need a visa to visit the UK, you apply for that visa as normal rather than an ETA; the two systems are separate.
  • Holders of existing UK immigration status — including a valid UK visa, Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR), settled or pre-settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme.
  • Legal residents of Ireland from a normally visa-exempt nationality (such as US or EU citizens), when travelling within the Common Travel Area and able to show proof of Irish residency.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Pricing

How Much Does a UK ETA Cost?

The official UK government fee for an ETA is £20, payable only through the official UK ETA app or GOV.UK. This fee is kept under periodic review by the Home Office, so it's worth checking the current rate on GOV.UK before you apply.

Watch Out for Third-Party Websites

A number of unofficial websites advertise UK ETA "processing services" and charge £40, £60 or more for the same authorisation. These sites add no speed and no better chance of approval — the Home Office decision timeline is identical whether you apply directly or through one of these agents. Always apply directly through the official UK ETA app or GOV.UK to avoid paying an unnecessary mark-up.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Step by Step

How to Apply for a UK ETA

The easiest way to apply is through the official UK ETA app, available on the Apple App Store and Google Play, though you can also apply via GOV.UK. To apply, you'll need to:

  1. Download the UK ETA app or go to the official application page on GOV.UK.
  2. Provide your contact details and passport information.
  3. Upload a valid digital photo, meeting the same standards as UK passport photos.
  4. Answer a short set of suitability and criminality questions.
  5. Pay the £20 fee.
  6. Wait for your decision — most applicants are approved automatically within minutes.

Important: you must travel using the exact same passport you used when applying for your ETA, since the authorisation is digitally linked to that specific document.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Timing

How Long Does It Take to Get Approved?

Most applicants currently receive an automatic decision within minutes of applying through the UK ETA app. However, the Home Office recommends applying at least three working days before you travel, to allow time for the small number of applications that require further manual review.

Practical advice: don't leave your ETA application until the day of travel. While most decisions are instant, applying a few days ahead gives you a safety margin if your application is one of the exceptions that needs closer review.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Duration

How Long Does a UK ETA Last?

Once approved, a UK ETA is valid for two years, or until your passport expires — whichever happens first. During that validity period, it permits multiple separate trips to the UK, with each individual stay allowed to last up to six months.

This means frequent visitors don't need to reapply before every trip — one approved ETA covers repeat visits within the two-year window, as long as you're travelling on the same passport you applied with.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

If you're eligible for an ETA, you cannot choose to apply for a visa instead, and there's no benefit in doing so — the ETA route is faster and cheaper. If your ETA application is refused, or if you have circumstances you'd like to explain in more detail (such as a previous refusal or criminal record), applying for a Standard Visitor Visa instead gives you the opportunity to provide that context directly.

If Something Goes Wrong

What Happens If Your ETA Is Refused?

If your application is rejected (a technical or administrative issue), you'll be told the reason and can apply again. If your application is refused (a substantive decision against granting permission), you cannot appeal — instead, you would need to apply for a UK visa if you still wish to travel.

Common reasons an ETA application may not succeed include a criminal history, previous UK immigration violations, or information suggesting the stated purpose of the visit isn't accurate. The Home Office does not always provide detailed reasons for a refusal.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Connecting Flights

Do You Need an ETA Just to Transit Through the UK?

It depends on your route. Eligible visitors taking a connecting flight who pass through UK passport control need an ETA, even if they're not leaving the airport for a full visit. However, passengers transiting through Heathrow or Manchester airports who do not go through UK passport control do not currently need an ETA.

If you have a layover at a different UK airport, or your connection requires you to clear passport control, check your specific itinerary carefully — this is one of the most commonly misunderstood parts of the scheme.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Special Case

What About Dual British Citizens?

Dual citizens who hold British citizenship do not need an ETA and cannot apply for one — but they must travel to the UK using a valid British passport or a Certificate of Entitlement, not their other nationality's passport. This has been a significant change for some dual citizens who previously travelled on a non-British passport.

If you're a dual citizen and unsure which document to travel on, or believe you may hold British citizenship automatically without realising it, GOV.UK provides detailed guidance for this specific situation, including options for those who've settled in the UK under the EU Settlement Scheme.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.

Stay Safe

Avoiding Fraudulent ETA Websites

Because the ETA scheme is relatively new and involves a real government fee, it has attracted scam and lookalike websites that charge inflated prices or, in worse cases, attempt to steal personal and passport information.

  • Only apply through the official UK ETA app or GOV.UK directly.
  • Be wary of any site charging significantly more than the £20 government fee.
  • If you believe you've been the victim of an immigration-related scam, you can report it directly through GOV.UK's official reporting tool.
  • You can verify a valid ETA status at any time using the official "Check My ETA" service on GOV.UK.

Source: GOV.UK / Home Office ETA factsheet, April 2026.
