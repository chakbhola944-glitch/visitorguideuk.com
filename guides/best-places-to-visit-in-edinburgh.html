10 Best Places to Visit in Edinburgh

Scotland8 min read

Edinburgh packs an unusual amount of history and scenery into a compact, walkable centre.

Split between the medieval Old Town and the elegant Georgian New Town, Edinburgh is one of the more rewarding UK cities to explore on foot. The following ten places give a strong overview of the city, from its best-known landmark to a quieter, less-visited corner.

01

Edinburgh Castle

Perched on an extinct volcanic rock above the city, Edinburgh Castle is Scotland's most visited paid attraction, home to the Scottish Crown Jewels and the Stone of Destiny.

Book tickets in advance, particularly in summer, since it's consistently one of the busiest sites in the city.

02

The Royal Mile

This is the historic spine of the Old Town, running from the castle down to the Palace of Holyroodhouse, lined with closes, shops and historic buildings.

Wandering slightly off the main street into the narrow closes often reveals quieter, equally atmospheric corners.

03

Arthur's Seat

The main peak of an extinct volcano group in Holyrood Park, Arthur's Seat offers a genuinely rewarding climb with sweeping views over the whole city and out to the Firth of Forth.

The ascent takes most visitors around an hour to ninety minutes round trip, depending on pace and route.

04

The Palace of Holyroodhouse

The official royal residence in Scotland sits at the foot of the Royal Mile, with state rooms open to visitors alongside the ruins of the adjoining Holyrood Abbey.

05

The National Museum of Scotland

A large museum covering Scottish history, world cultures and natural science, with free general admission and an impressive glass-roofed entrance hall.

06

Calton Hill

A short climb near the east end of the city centre, Calton Hill is dotted with monuments and offers one of the best skyline views over Edinburgh at a fraction of the effort required for Arthur's Seat.

07

The Grassmarket

A historic market square beneath the castle, now home to pubs, restaurants and independent shops, with dramatic views up towards Edinburgh Castle.

08

The Scottish National Gallery

A significant collection of European and Scottish art in a neoclassical building on the Mound, with free general admission.

09

Dean Village

A quiet, picturesque former milling village on the Water of Leith, a short walk from the New Town but feeling far removed from the city centre's crowds.

10

The Royal Botanic Garden

A large botanic garden a short bus ride from the centre, with free general admission to the outdoor gardens and glasshouses covering plant life from around the world.

Planning Your Edinburgh Visit

These ten places can realistically be spread across two to three days without rushing, with the castle, Royal Mile and Arthur's Seat forming a natural core for a shorter visit. Edinburgh's compact centre means most of these sights are within walking distance of each other, particularly those in and around the Old Town.

If you only have a single day, prioritising Edinburgh Castle, a walk down the Royal Mile and a climb up either Arthur's Seat or the more modest Calton Hill will give a strong sense of the city.

FAQ

Common Questions

Edinburgh Castle is the city's most iconic and most visited attraction, though Arthur's Seat offers the best combination of scenery and free access.

A focused day can cover the castle, the Royal Mile and either Arthur's Seat or Calton Hill, though two to three days allows a much more relaxed pace.

It's a genuine hike rather than a gentle stroll, but it's manageable for most reasonably fit visitors in around an hour each way.
