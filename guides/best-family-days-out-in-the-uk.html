Family Travel

Best Family Days Out in the UK

Family Travel8 min read

Updated October 2026

The UK offers plenty of family-friendly attractions that go well beyond standard sightseeing — from hands-on, free-to-enter museums to full theme parks that can fill an entire day.

Whether you're after hands-on history, a full day of rides, or somewhere free to spend a rainy afternoon, the UK's family attractions span every budget and every corner of the country. Below are seven of the best, chosen for their mix of entertainment and value.

01

National Railway Museum, York

Free entryYorkAges 3+

A large, free-to-enter museum in York tracing the history of Britain's railways, with real locomotives that children can explore up close. Highlights include the Mallard (the world's fastest steam locomotive) and the Royal carriages. Well suited to children of all ages, and a good rainy-day option.

02

JORVIK Viking Centre, York

PaidYorkAges 5+

An immersive reconstruction of Viking-age York, built on the site of a genuine archaeological excavation. Children ride through a recreated Viking village with sounds, smells and life-size figures. Popular with curious young visitors — book ahead in school holidays.

03

Cadbury World, Birmingham

PaidBirminghamAges 4+

An attraction dedicated to the history of chocolate-making at the Cadbury factory in Bournville, combining history with an inevitably popular subject for kids. Includes a 4D cinema, a demonstration of chocolate-making, and — of course — plenty of opportunities to sample the product.

04

Science and Industry Museum, Manchester

Free entryManchesterAges 4+

Hands-on exhibits covering Manchester's industrial and scientific history, free to enter and well suited to curious young visitors. Highlights include working steam engines, an interactive Experiment gallery, and the world's oldest surviving passenger railway station.

05

Museum of Liverpool

Free entryLiverpoolAll ages

Interactive galleries covering the city's social history and culture, free to visit and set on Liverpool's family-friendly waterfront. Includes exhibits on The Beatles, Liverpool's football heritage, and the city's role in global trade and migration.

06

LEGOLAND Windsor

PaidWindsorAges 3-12

A theme park built around LEGO, located near Windsor and popular with younger children in particular. Features rides, live shows, miniature LEGO landmarks built from millions of bricks, and a dedicated Duplo Valley for toddlers. Book online for cheaper tickets.

07

National Museum of Scotland, Edinburgh

Free entryEdinburghAll ages

Wide-ranging galleries including natural science exhibits, free to enter and centrally located in Edinburgh. The Explore! gallery and the roof terrace are especially popular with families, and the museum's Victorian building is worth a visit on its own.

Planning a Family Trip to the UK

Cities with compact, walkable centres — such as York, Bath and Edinburgh — tend to be easier to manage with children than destinations that require a lot of transport between sights. Mixing free museums with a small amount of paid, more active entertainment, such as a theme park day, often gives the best balance for a family trip.

Many attractions offer family tickets that reduce the overall cost compared with buying individual entries — typically 10-25% off for 2 adults and 2 children. It's worth checking the attraction's website before you book.

If you're travelling by train, the Family & Friends Railcard gives 1/3 off adult fares and 60% off children's fares. Before you book, use our Train Ticket Price Calculator to compare fares between UK cities, and our Hotel Cost Estimator to budget for accommodation.

Free vs Paid Family Attractions

One of the best things about family travel in the UK is that so many of the top attractions are free to enter. National museums in London, York, Manchester, Liverpool and Edinburgh don't charge admission — you only pay for travel and food. That makes a family trip to a city like York or Manchester surprisingly affordable.

Paid attractions like LEGOLAND Windsor, Cadbury World and JORVIK Viking Centre cost £15-£60 per person, but they offer bigger, more active experiences. A balanced family trip often mixes both — free museums in the mornings, one paid highlight in the afternoon.

Practical

Quick Tips

  • Look for family tickets, which are often 10-25% cheaper than buying individual admissions.
  • Balance museum visits with outdoor time to help manage energy levels across a full day.
  • Check accessibility for buggies if visiting historic sites with steps or uneven surfaces.
  • Book popular attractions in advance during school holidays — JORVIK and LEGOLAND sell out.
  • Travel midweek during term time if you can — attractions are quieter and often cheaper.
  • Pack a picnic for free museums, many of which have outdoor space for lunch.
