Best Free Things to Do in London

London7 min read

London has a reputation as an expensive city, but a surprising number of its best experiences don't cost anything at all.

Thanks to a long-standing UK policy of free admission to national museums, London is home to some of the best free museums anywhere in the world. Add in public parks, markets and viewpoints that cost nothing to enjoy, and it's entirely possible to spend several rewarding days in the capital without paying a single entry fee.

01

The British Museum

The British Museum holds one of the world's most extensive collections of art and antiquities, including the Rosetta Stone and the Parthenon sculptures, and general admission is free.

The museum is large enough to reward repeat visits, so first-timers are often better off focusing on two or three galleries rather than trying to see everything in one trip.

02

The National Gallery

Overlooking Trafalgar Square, the National Gallery houses a major collection of Western European painting from the 13th to the 19th century, with free entry to its permanent collection.

It's an easy stop to combine with a walk around Trafalgar Square and the surrounding streets.

03

Tate Modern

Housed in a former power station on the South Bank, Tate Modern is one of the world's largest museums of modern and contemporary art, with free access to its permanent collection.

The building itself, including the vast Turbine Hall, is worth seeing regardless of your interest in the art on display.

04

The Natural History Museum

Famous for its dinosaur skeletons and striking Romanesque building, the Natural History Museum in South Kensington offers free general admission to its main galleries.

It's particularly popular with families, so early mornings or weekdays tend to be quieter.

05

The Victoria and Albert Museum

The V&A is the world's largest museum of applied and decorative arts and design, with free entry to its permanent collections covering everything from fashion to sculpture.

It sits within walking distance of the Natural History Museum, making South Kensington a strong base for a museum-focused day.

06

A Walk Along the South Bank

The riverside path along the South Bank offers free views of landmarks including the London Eye, the Globe Theatre and St Paul's Cathedral across the water.

Street performers, secondhand book stalls and food markets along the route add extra interest without any admission cost.

07

Hampstead Heath

This large area of parkland in north London offers wide open spaces, ponds and one of the best free viewpoints over the city skyline from Parliament Hill.

It's a good option for visitors wanting a break from the busier central sights.

08

Borough Market (Browsing)

While buying food naturally costs money, simply walking through Borough Market near London Bridge — one of the city's oldest food markets — is free and gives a strong sense of London's food culture.

Making the Most of Free London

With several major museums offering free entry, a first-time visitor can build an entire itinerary around genuinely world-class attractions without spending on admission. Combining museum visits with free outdoor spaces like the South Bank or Hampstead Heath makes it possible to see a real cross-section of London at very little cost.

Keep in mind that while general admission is free at these museums, special or touring exhibitions often carry a separate charge, so it's worth checking in advance if there's a specific exhibition you want to see.

Practical

Quick Tips

  • Free museums still welcome donations at the entrance if you'd like to contribute.
  • Visit major museums early on weekday mornings to avoid the busiest periods.
  • Check each museum's website for any ticketed special exhibitions before you go.
  • Combine two nearby free museums, such as the Natural History Museum and V&A, into a single day.
