Where to Stay

Practical guidance on choosing the right area to base yourself in, whatever UK city you're visiting.

Overview

Choosing Where to Stay

In most UK cities, staying within or close to the historic centre makes the biggest difference to your trip, since it reduces reliance on transport and puts you within walking distance of major sights. This is especially true in compact cities like York, Bath and Oxford, where the historic core can genuinely be covered on foot.

In larger cities such as London, Manchester or Edinburgh, staying near a well-connected transport hub — a central train station or tram line — is often more practical than trying to stay directly in the busiest tourist areas, which can be more expensive without offering much practical benefit.

More to Consider

General Accommodation Tips

Central vs Outer Areas

Central accommodation costs more but usually saves on transport time and cost, especially for short stays.

Transport Links

In larger cities, staying near a train station or tram stop is often more useful than staying in the busiest district.

Booking Timing

Prices generally rise closer to popular dates, such as festivals or school holidays, so booking early helps.

Self-Catering Options

Apartments and self-catering stays can reduce costs for longer trips or family visits.

Practical

Tips for Where to Stay

  • Compare the cost of a slightly less central stay against the transport savings of walking everywhere.
  • Check whether your accommodation is within the historic centre or walls in cities like York, since this affects how much walking you'll do.
  • Read recent reviews for noise levels if staying in a busy nightlife area of any city.
  • Confirm cancellation policies before booking, particularly for trips planned well in advance.
