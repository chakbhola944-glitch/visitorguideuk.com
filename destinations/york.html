Top Things to Do in York

York's compact centre makes it easy to explore on foot. These are the highlights you shouldn't miss.

1. York Minster

One of the largest Gothic cathedrals in Northern Europe, York Minster dominates the skyline. Climb the Central Tower for panoramic views (be prepared for a narrow spiral staircase), or explore the Undercroft to see Roman and Norman remains.

2. The Shambles

This preserved medieval street with its overhanging timber-framed buildings is often cited as the inspiration for Diagon Alley in Harry Potter. It's incredibly photogenic, especially early in the morning before the crowds arrive. Don't miss the Shambles Market nearby for local produce and street food.

3. Walk the City Walls

York has the most complete set of medieval city walls in England. The full circuit is about 2 miles (3.4 km) and takes roughly 1.5 to 2 hours. It offers unique views of the city and the Minster. You can join the walls at various points, including near the train station or Bootham Bar.

4. Jorvik Viking Centre

Built on the site of a major Viking excavation, this immersive museum takes you through a recreation of a 10th-century Viking street. It's popular with families and history buffs alike. Booking in advance is recommended during peak season.

5. National Railway Museum

Located near the train station, this is the world's largest railway museum and it's free to enter. It houses iconic locomotives like Mallard and a Japanese Shinkansen bullet train. It's an excellent option if you're looking for free activities. You can find more free museums across the UK on our Free UK Museums & Attractions Map.

Suggested One-Day Itinerary

If you only have one day, here's a realistic plan:

  • Morning (9:00 AM): Start with a walk on the City Walls, entering near the station. Head towards Bootham Bar and walk to the Minster.
  • Late Morning (10:30 AM): Visit York Minster. If you want to climb the tower, allow an extra 45 minutes.
  • Lunch (12:30 PM): Head to the Shambles Market or a nearby pub for a classic Sunday roast or a Yorkshire pudding wrap.
  • Afternoon (2:00 PM): Explore the Shambles and the surrounding independent shops. Then visit the Jorvik Viking Centre (pre-booked).
  • Late Afternoon (4:00 PM): If time allows, visit the National Railway Museum (free) or relax in the Museum Gardens.

Where to Eat & Drink

York has a strong food scene, from historic pubs to modern independents.

  • Bettys Café Tea Rooms: A Yorkshire institution for afternoon tea. Expect queues, but it's worth it for the experience.
  • The Shambles Kitchen: Famous for its pulled pork and Yorkshire pudding wraps.
  • House of the Trembling Madness: A quirky, historic pub with an excellent selection of Belgian and local beers.
  • For a quick bite: Look for small bakeries selling fresh Yorkshire pasties and sausage rolls.

Practical Tips for Your Visit

Transport

York is easily reachable by train from London (approx. 2 hours), Edinburgh (2.5 hours), and Manchester (1.5 hours). The station is a 10-minute walk from the city centre. Once in the centre, everything is walkable.
