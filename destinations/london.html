Overview

Planning a Trip to London

London rewards visitors who slow down and pick a few areas to explore properly rather than rushing between landmarks. The South Bank, Westminster and the West End cover most of the well-known sights within walking distance of each other, while areas like Shoreditch, Camden and Greenwich offer a different, more local pace.

The city is well served by the London Underground, so most attractions are a short journey apart even when they feel worlds apart in character. A contactless bank card or an Oyster card is the simplest way to pay for travel, and daily spending is automatically capped once you have paid enough for a day pass.

FAQ

Common Questions About Visiting London

How many days do you need in London? +

Most first-time visitors are comfortable with 3 to 4 days, which allows time for the main museums, a river cruise, one or two markets, and a West End show without rushing between areas.

Is London expensive to visit? +

London can be visited on a range of budgets. Many major museums are free to enter, and public transport with an Oyster or contactless card is cheaper than individual tickets, though accommodation and dining costs are higher than in most other UK cities.

What is the best way to get around London? +

The Underground (Tube) is the fastest way to cover longer distances, while buses are a cheaper and scenic option for shorter trips. Walking is often quickest in central areas like the West End and the City.
