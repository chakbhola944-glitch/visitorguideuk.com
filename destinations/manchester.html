Overview

Planning a Trip to Manchester

Manchester grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution as a centre of textile manufacturing, and that history is still visible in the warehouses, canals and civic buildings that now house museums, bars and independent shops across the city centre.

The compact centre is easy to explore on foot, with the Northern Quarter's independent shops and street art a short walk from the grand Victorian architecture around Albert Square and the modern developments at Spinningfields and MediaCityUK in nearby Salford.

Must-See

Top Attractions in Manchester

The landmarks most visitors build their itinerary around.

Science and Industry Museum

A large museum on the site of the world's oldest passenger railway station, covering Manchester's industrial and scientific history.

Manchester Cathedral

A medieval cathedral in the heart of the city centre, known for its wide nave and Victorian stained glass.

John Rylands Library

A striking neo-Gothic library on Deansgate, home to rare books and manuscripts.

Northern Quarter

Manchester's creative district, known for independent shops, street art, cafes and music venues.

National Football Museum

A museum dedicated to the history of English football, housed in the striking Urbis building.

MediaCityUK & Salford Quays

A modern waterside development a short tram ride from the centre, home to broadcasters and public art.

Budget-Friendly

Free Things to Do in Manchester

Some of the best experiences in Manchester don't cost anything.

Manchester Art Gallery

A significant collection of British art with free general admission, in the city centre.

Science and Industry Museum

Most of the museum's galleries covering Manchester's industrial history are free to enter.

John Rylands Library

The library's atmospheric reading room and exhibitions are free to visit.

Manchester Cathedral

The cathedral is free to enter and sits a short walk from the main shopping streets.

Beyond the City

Best Day Trips from Manchester

Easy journeys for visitors with an extra day to spare.

Peak District

A national park of moorland and villages within about an hour of the city by train or car.

Chester

A historic walled Roman city with a well-preserved medieval centre, under an hour away by train.

Liverpool

Easily reached by train in around 45 minutes, making a Manchester–Liverpool combined trip practical.

Lake District

England's largest national park is a longer day trip, best suited to visitors with a car or organised tour.

Practical

Manchester Travel Tips

  • The Metrolink tram network is the easiest way to reach areas like Salford Quays and MediaCityUK from the centre.
  • Manchester's compact city centre is very walkable, so a day pass for transport is often unnecessary if you're staying central.
  • As with much of northern England, rain is common; pack a waterproof layer whatever the season.
  • Manchester Piccadilly is the main railway station, with good connections to London, Liverpool, Leeds and beyond.
